# Game style

- Franco-Prussian War era.
- Style is steampunk adjacent, probably more fantasy-ish.
- Inspired by magical realism vibes.
- Currently, most of this style is limited to art style and naming.
- It's possible mechanics will be introduced to further this style.

# Story

- Each player plays as a societal movement within their assigned nation.
- This movement has political, economic, and religious beliefs.
- The movement gains promenince in said nation.
- The movement seeks to bring success to their nation in alignment with thier movement's political, economic, and religious beliefs.
- The movements first gain internal coherence (Phase I, card drafting).
- They then ascend to power within their nation and shape it in accordance with thier values (Phase II, internal buildup).
- Finally, the movement guides the nation through international relations of various kinds, such as taking of territory, exchange of resources, spreading of religion, making treaties, etc. (Phase III)

# Turns

- In phases 2-3, players start with three *Action Pools* 1 Build, 1 Move, and 1 Recruit. In phase 3, players also start with a fourth *Action Pool*, 1 Interact.
- Players begin with a hand of cards.
	- The number of cards is configurable, as this will be an important thing to tweak.
    - Before the first turn in phase 2, all players draw their first hand.
    - Cards on a player's nation mat are playable as if from their hand.
- Any players who's Political, Enconomic, or Religion cards have *Action Pool* bonuses (like +1 Move) can apply said bonuses to their pool.
- At the beginning of the term, any resources generated by production facilites are automatically available in the *Resource Pool*. Only Tier I resources are available automatically.
- Before consuming any actions in the *Action Pool*, players must pay any maintenance costs (e.g. paying the associated resource cost for maintaining active army units).
- Cards may be played from a player's hand or nation mat. Cards that provide resources may be played in order to fulfull maintenace costs.
- Resources from production facilities that are not available automatically (such as those from Tier II faclilities) may be generated in order to fulfill maintenance costs. This may involve consuming Tier I resources from the *Resource Pool* in order to generate Tier II resource to be added to the *Resource Pool*.
- Similiarly, resources from trading posts can be claimed to fulfill maintenance costs.
- If maintenance costs are not able to be fulfilled, the player must take the necessary actions (such as dissolving undersupplied military units).
- Once the maintenace phase is complete, players can now begin expending their actions.
- Once a player's actions are depleted and/or they are unable to do any more actions, the may:
	- Place up to one card from their hand onto their nation mat. If their nation mat is full, they may discard up to one card from the mat (the ability to do this should be toggleable in configuration).
    - Claim up to one unspent resource as a token and add it to their token reserves.
- Finally, a player discards all cards from their play area and any unplayed cards in their hand. They then draw a new hand.

# Actions

## Build

- *Build* allows a player to construct *Works*.
- One *Works* can be constructed per *Build* available
- *Works* include:
	- Production facilities
    - Railroads
    - Embassies
    - Trading posts
    - Ports
    - Ships
    - Defenses
- In order to build works:
	- The build conditions must be met.
    - The associated costs must be paid.
- Once constructed, *Works* remain on the board uness actively removed, such as via military conflict.

## Move

- *Move* allows a player to move *Units*.
- Each *Unit* type is moved in its own way.
- *Unit* types include:
	- Army
    - Missionary
    - Ship (either merchant or naval)
- A *Unit* must be moved in accordance to the rules of its type.

## Recruit

- *Recruit* allows Missionary units, reserves, and population modifiers to be placed.
- A Missionary unit can be recruited from a country's capital or any tile that has been fully converted to the player's nation's religion. The number of missionaries that can be recruited per nation is capped (cap configurable).
- Reserves are recruited from a tile based on that tile's population. The number of reserves a tile can generate per recruitment are determined by a ratio of population to recruits, rounded down (the numbers are configurable). This can only be done on tiles of your own color that are not controlled by another nation. Reserves accumulate on a tile, and can be converted into Army units.
- Population modifiers are token placed on a tile that affect that tile's population in some way. They can be placed within tiles of your own color that are not controlled by other nations. Each terrain tile type has a set number of modifiers that can be added. Once a modifier is placed, it cannot be removed or replaced with a different tile. Here are the current types:
	- *Population Boom* increases the tile's population by a certain amount (configurable, limited number of tokens, probably some tokens increase population by more than others).
    - *Production Capacity* increases the number of production facilities that can be produced by one (total number of tokens configurable).
    - *Worker Efficiency* allows one production facility to produce double output once per turn (total number of tokens configurable).
    - *Militarized* improves the ratio of population:recruits (ratio configurable, available number of tokens configurable).
    - *Hardy Population* increases the terrain's basic defense number (configurable, number of tokens configurable).
    - There many be more in the future, that's what I have for now.
 
## Interaction

- *Interaction* allows to take various actions that interact directly with other players.
- The *Interaction* types are:
	- Battle
    - Influence
    - Diplomacy
- **Battle**:
	- A battle can be initiated in any tile where you have at least one army unit and there is at least one enemy work or army unit.
    - The number of Army units the attacking and defending players have in that tile is the maximum number of hits they can deal. Card modifiers can change this.
    - Each side rolls a die (sidedness is configurable, 10-sided is the default).
    - The higher number die goes to the attacker, the lower to the defender.
    - Each side's attack points are based on the number of their die roll, capped by the number of Army units they have.
    - Defenders deal damage directly to attacking Army units.
    - Attackers' damage is first absorbed by the terrain's *Ruggedness* (currently this attribute is called movement, this will need to be changed).
    - Attackers' damager is then absorbed by Defense Works, each type of which can absorbe a certain number of hits. Expendable Defense Works are destroyed during this phase, Permenant Defense Works are not.
    - Remaining damage is directed first at enemy Army units (one hit kill), and once those are cleared, at any other works located on the tile (also one hit kill). Permenant Defense Works can be destroyed at this phase with a single hit.
    - Destroyed Army units are returned to their respective color's supply.
- **Influence**: There are two types of influence that can be spread in this way:
	- *Trade* influence can be spread by placing a trade token in the capital of a non-hostile country. This allows Embassy Works to be built. Once an embassy has been built, trade tokens can be spread to any tile of that nation's color that they control, allowing Trading Post Works to be built.
    - *Military* influence can be spread by placing an influence tokens 1) in a tile adjacent to a tile you control, or 2) in a tile where Army units of your color are the only units occupying said tile.
- **Diplomacy**: Involves various kinds of diplomatic relations.
	- *Treaty Talks* initiate a treaty proposals between two nations. Two basic treaty stipulations can be agreed to:
    	- *Trade Agreements* allow for mutal Port usage by merchant ships and the building of Trading Posts without he need for Trade Influence. Both countries must have embassies in each others' capital tiles for this stipulation to be agreed to.
        - *Military Alliances* allow for mutual Port usage by naval ships and bind each other to military aid. The latter means that if militarily allied Army units are present in the same tile, then attack and defend together. This also means that each others' territories are considered same-color for purposes of Army unit supply.
	- *Protectorate Proposals* may be initiated by either the Protector state or the Protected state. If both sides agree to the proposal, the Protector state will have proxy access to all of the Protected state's treaty agreements and can treat the Protected state's color and controlled tiles as their own for Army unit supply purposes. The Protected state cannot be attacked by the Protector state.

# Units

## Army

- Army units constitute a nation's active army.

### Activation

- Army units are activated from reserves.
- A certain number of reserves can be converted into a single army unit (this is configurable).
- An army unit can be dissolved back into a certain number of reserves (configurable):
	- The army unit must be in a tile of its own nation's color.
    - The tile must not be controlled by another nation.
    
### Movement

- There are three ways an army can move:
	- March
    - Rail
    - Ship
- **March**: Move any number of your Army units from one tile to an adjacent tile. If the tile you move from 1) has no remaining Army units of your color, 2) is not a tile of your own color, and 3) is not controlled by you, then you may place down a Supply Line (a special piece). If you do not have any Supply Lines left, you cannot place one.
- **Rail**: Move up to a maximum number (configurable) of your Army units from one tile adjacent to a railroad to another tile adjacent to a railroad, provided the following conditions are met:
	- The railroads are connected by a continuous chain of railroads. The railroads may be of any nation's color.
    - All railroads in the chain are adjacent to at least one tile you control.
    - No railroads in the chain have been sabatoged.
- **Ship**: Move up to a maximum nubmer (configurable) of your Army units from a tile you control that has a Port to a land tile adjacent to one of your naval ships. After moving the units, the naval ship has "expended" its transport ability and cannot be used to more Army units until after it returns to a port you control.

### Maintenence
- Various Army unit thresholds require a certain amount of resources for maintenence, e.g. 1-4 units requires a certain set of resources, 5-10 units requires another set of resources, etc. The army unit thresholds and the resource cost of each threshold are configurable.
- If a nation does not have enough resources to resupply its active military at its current threshold, Army units must be either dissolved into reserves (if conditions are met) or disbanded.
- Additionally, all Army units must be able to receive supplies. Units are able to receive supplies if:
	- They are located in a tile of the nation's color, regardless of if a different nation controls the tile.
    - They are located in or adjacent to a tile they control, regardless of the tile's color.
    - They are able to connect to *a tile either of their color that they control* or *with at least one of their naval ships* via a connected chain of 1) tiles with Army units of their color, 2) tiles with Supply Lines of their color, 3) railroads that are adjacent to tiles they control, or 3) railroads that are not adjacent to tiles occupied by hostile Army units of other colors (Army units from allied nations do not count as hostile). Railroads that have been sabatoged cannot be used.
- Army units that are not able to receive supplies have a set number of turns (configurable) to reconnect to their supplies before they must be disbanded.
- When an Army unit is disbanded, it is moved back into the player's supply and is not converted into reserves.
